# The EC66 (EA66) and its controller

What the robot we drive is, how its controller behaves, and what its SDK
exposes: the reference for the driver's design (`docs/design.md`). It is not a
service manual: it keeps what matters to software that drives the arm, in
enough detail to reason about it. Written 2026-10-21 from the vendor's
manuals, our own measurements, and our inferences, each marked.

Our robot is an EA66, the EC66 sold under the Annuo name: the Chinese SDK
manual is Annuo's edition of Elite's (same calls, same text) [S-zh cover]
[I]. Its controller runs firmware V3.15.3 [M] and sits at 192.168.0.200. The
3.19.2 manuals are newer than the firmware; calls added after 3.11.2 are
marked where they matter.

---

## 1. Summary

- The EC66 is a 6-axis cobot: 6 kg payload, 914 mm reach, ±0.03 mm
  repeatability [D]. Its control box is a complete robot controller: it
  plans and interpolates every kind of motion, solves the kinematics, runs a
  dynamics model, implements the certified safety functions, and runs
  programs (JBI), Lua scripts, a soft PLC and fieldbus slaves [U 3, 6-10] [C].
- The servo drives are in the joints. The box feeds them 48 V and talks to
  them over a field bus on one RS-485 pair [Sv 3.2] [I]. A PC never reaches
  a joint: it talks to the controller.
- Each joint has two encoders (a magnetic one on the output side, a motor
  encoder with an index pulse) plus Hall sensors, and keeps its multi-turn
  count in flash [Sv 4, 5]. This is why start-up has an "imprecise to
  precise" step and a "synchronise" step (§3.4).
- A key switch on the pendant decides who may move the arm: TEACH (the
  pendant), PLAY (programs started on the pendant), REMOTE (the SDK). SDK
  motion works in REMOTE only [U 6.2] [S].
- The SDK is four TCP ports: 8055 commands (JSON-RPC 2.0), 8056 a state frame
  every 8 ms, 8058 and 8059 the controller's own logs [S 1] [S-zh 1].
- Safety belongs to the controller: emergency stop (stop category 1),
  safeguard stop (category 2), collision detection, power, momentum, force
  and speed limits, reduced mode, soft limits [U 7.4, 12.2]. Nothing on the
  PC can add to it.
- Speeds of the types VJ (% of the joints' maximum), V (mm/s) and VR
  (deg/s) are scaled by the global speed slider (V+/V− on the pendant,
  `setSpeed`); the "absolute" types AV and AVR are not [J 2.4].
- Learned on our robot [M]: some answers take well over 50 ms; bursts of
  unconfirmed commands can wedge the controller until a power cycle; the
  rotation convention is R = Rz·Ry·Rx (§7).

## 2. The system

```
  PC ── Ethernet ──┬─ 8055 commands (JSON-RPC)     8058 log, 8059 raw log
                   ├─ 8056 state frames (8 ms)     502 Modbus TCP, Profinet, EtherNet/IP (slaves)
                   │
  ┌────────────────┴─────────────── control box ─────────────────────────────┐
  │ core board: Linux; motion controller (planning, interpolation,             │
  │   kinematics, dynamics); safety logic; JBI, Lua, soft PLC; SDK servers;    │
  │   web pendant (6680), VNC, sFTP (/rbctrl)                                  │
  │ PAD IO board: safety inputs (E-stop, safeguard, dual channel), DI/DO/AI/AO │
  │ supplies: mains → 48 V bus for the joints, 24 V I/O, 5 V; brake plate      │
  │   (braking resistors for energy fed back by the joints); clock battery     │
  └──────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
         │ pendant: display, E-stop, enabling switch,    │ robot cable (≤ 6 m)
         │ key switch TEACH/PLAY/REMOTE                  │ 48 V + RS-485 bus
         ▼                                               ▼
     ERP300 pendant          J1 ─ J2 ─ J3 ─ J4 ─ J5 ─ J6 ─ tool flange
                             each joint: drive, motor + Halls, reducer,   (2 DI, 2 DO, AI, AO,
                             brake, joint encoder, motor encoder          RS-485, 2 buttons, LEDs)
```

### 2.1 Parts and variants

- Arm, control box and pendant, joined by three cables: mains, the robot
  cable (at most 6 m), the pendant's [U 3, 5.4.2].
- Two control boxes exist [D] [Sv 5-2]:
  - the standard box (ours): 90–264 VAC, or 19–72 VDC in the "M" version;
    16 DI, 20 DO (the datasheet's text says 16/16), 2 AI, 4 AO, IP44, 15 kg;
  - the optional "Mini" controller, a 45–55 VDC box of 205×135×40 mm and
    1.2 kg, IP40, 8 DI/8 DO, "for integrators, OEM partners and users who want
    to create their own custom design".

  Both run the same controller software: the SDK manual covers the Mini with
  smaller I/O ranges and one Mini-only call (the brake voltage) [S 2.2.5.1,
  2.2.2.89] [I]. Neither exposes the joints below the controller: the Mini is
  a smaller package, not a lower-level interface.
- The ERP300 pendant: 8" resistive touch screen, emergency stop, a
  three-position enabling switch on the back, the key switch, LEDs (power,
  alarm, servo), V+/V−, servo, start and pause buttons, jog keys [D] [U 3.3].
  Its screen is the controller's "web teach pendant", also served at
  http://<ip>:6680/ and over VNC [U Table 1, 11]. It can be marked
  "disconnected", which then requires an external emergency stop [U 11.4-11.5,
  12.3.2].
- Versions in the 3.19.2 manuals: software V3.19.2, servo V11.54.1,
  mechanical V4.B2.1, hardware V1.11.0 [U Table 1]. Ours: controller V3.15.3
  [M]; the joints' firmware is read with `getJointVersion` [S 2.2.8.2].

## 3. The arm

### 3.1 Geometry

| Joint | d [m] | a [m] | α |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | 0.096 | 0 | −π/2 |
| J2 | 0 | 0.418 | 0 |
| J3 | 0 | 0.398 | 0 |
| J4 | 0.122 | 0 | −π/2 |
| J5 | 0.098 | 0 | −π/2 |
| J6 | 0.089 | 0 | 0 |

DH parameters [U 3.1.2.2]; the controller reports them with `getDH` [S
2.2.2.29].

- The layout is Universal Robots' (three parallel axes J2, J3, J4; offset
  wrist) [I]. Singular configurations: the wrist (J5 at 0 or 180°), the
  elbow (J3 at 0 or 180°, the arm stretched or folded), and the shoulder (the
  wrist centre on the J1 axis: "the cylindrical space directly above and
  below the base is a singularity zone") [U 3.1.2.1] [I].
- Every joint turns ±360°; soft limits narrow that [U 3.1.2, 12.3.3].
- Speeds: J1–J2 150°/s, J3 190°/s, J4–J6 260°/s [D]. TCP speed: typically
  1.5 m/s [D], at most 2.8 m/s [U 14.6]. No accelerations are published.
- Payload 6 kg with its centre of gravity less than 260 mm from the flange
  axis; further out, less (a payload curve) [U 5.4.4].
- Any mounting angle, but the mount configuration (tilt α about X, then
  rotation γ about Z) must be set: gravity compensation, hand guiding and
  collision detection use it [U 5.4.1, 12.2.2].

### 3.2 Inside a joint

- Three sizes: "25" (J1, J2), "20" (J3), "14" (J4–J6) [Sv 5-1]; the joint
  firmware comes as j25, j20, j14 files [U 13.3.3].
- Each joint holds [Sv 3.2, 4, 5-1]:
  - a servo drive, in the joint [I: only 48 V and a bus pair enter a joint];
  - a motor with Hall sensors for commutation [Sv servo alarms 76-82];
  - a reducer ("the stop/start torque of the reducer" limits the EC66's tool
    force to 170 N) [J 2.8.5.1]; a harmonic drive, as usual in cobots [I];
  - an electromagnetic holding brake ("electromagnet assembly") [Sv 5-1];
  - two encoders: a joint (output-side) magnetic encoder ("magnetic encoder
    assembly"), absolute [I], and a motor encoder with A/B pulses and an
    index pulse that can be "contaminated", hence optical [I] [Sv servo alarms
    60-79]. The drive cross-checks them: "the data difference between joint
    and motor encoder is too large" [Sv 4.2];
  - a multi-turn ("multi circle") count kept in the joint's flash [Sv servo
    alarms 200-203];
  - temperature, current and bus-voltage monitoring (8056 reports joint
    temperatures; alarms for over-current, over-voltage, overheating) [S 3.1]
    [Sv 4.2].
- The SDK exposes both sides of the reducer: `get_joint_pos`, "the joint
  position of the robot output terminal", and `get_motor_pos`, "the joint
  position of the robot input" [S 2.2.2.3, 2.2.2.63]. Their difference is the
  reducer's twist under load [I].
- No joint torque sensors are documented. Torques are the motors' (per mille
  of rated) and estimates from the dynamics model, "the motor torque minus
  the torque required to drive itself" [S 2.2.2.14, 2.2.2.46]. Force control
  needs a 6-axis sensor at the flange, or uses the internal estimate
  [U 6.5.9] [I].

### 3.3 Brakes

- Released when the servo is switched on. The drive then checks them by
  turning the motor forward and back: alarms 90–92, "the holding brake ... is
  abnormal and the forward/reverse rotation of motor fails" [Sv 4.2] [I].
  That would be the small motion and the second or so a servo-on takes [I];
  the vendor's examples wait 1 s after `set_servo_status` [S 2.2.1.2, 2.3.1].
- Engaged with the servo off, on an emergency stop, on power loss. "The robot
  arm may slowly fall a short distance while the system brakes engage"
  [U 1.7]; sliding too far after closing raises alarm 94 [Sv 4.2].
- Released by hand without power: remove the joint's cap, push the brake
  down, turn the joint [Sv 3.2.2]. Backdrive mode releases each joint's brake
  while it is pushed: for moving the arm out of a collision or a soft limit
  after power-on [Sv 2.1.2] [U 6.5.7] [S 2.2.11].

### 3.4 Encoders and the start-up states

Two encoders per joint explain two separate start-up steps.

**Precise.** At power-on the arm is "uninitialised" and "imprecise"; the
pendant shows "stop(Imprecise)" [U 4.3, 6.5.10.1]. Initialising (hold the
enabling switch, press "Imprecise") and calibrating the encoders makes it
precise; "if it failed to finish the calibration, please manually move each
axis until the robot is in the precise mode" [U 4.3, 5.5, 6.1]. Read with
`get_servo_precise_position_status` (or the virtual bits M472 precise, M473
initialised); triggered by `calibrate_encoder_zero_position` (with the servo
on), PLC signal S22 or M41 [S 2.2.2.64, 2.2.3.21] [U 10, 14.1].
[I] The joint encoder gives each joint's absolute position at power-on, but
coarsely; the precise position needs the motor encoder's index pulse, which
appears only once the motor has turned a little. That fits the "no motor
encoder index signal detected" alarm and the advice to move each axis.

**Synchronised.** `syncMotorStatus`, "synchronise servo encoder data", with
`getMotorStatus` reading it (8056: `can_motor_run`) [S 2.2.1.3, 2.2.1.5]
[S 3.1]. It is needed after power-on and after an emergency stop: "turn the
emergency stop button to perform reset and synchronization operations"
[Sv servo alarm 40]; the PLC example clears the alarm "and synchronises" in
one press [U 10.1]. [I] It loads the drives' current positions into the
controller, so that its planner starts from where the arm really is: the arm
may have moved under its brakes, or by hand, while the controller was not
commanding it.

**Remembered.** The last shutdown position is stored and compared at the
next start (alarm 0-1030-4F, "the last shutdown position of joint %s is
different from the current position"); turning a joint more than 160° while
unpowered can lose it [Sv 3.2.2, 4.1].

**Ready.** M476, "normal state: 1 indicates that the robot is ready to
switch on the servo" [U 14.1].

After replacing a joint or the controller, or a heavy collision, the zero
positions may need the 20-point calibration [U 6.12].

## 4. The control box

### 4.1 Hardware

- Core board (the main computer), PAD IO board (+ expansion board), brake
  plate with braking resistors (it takes the energy the joints feed back into
  the 48 V bus: "bus voltage is overvoltage, please check whether the brake
  plate is connected"), DC/DC supplies (48 V, 24 V, 5 V), fans with two
  filters, a coin battery, a limit switch [Sv 3.3, 5-2] [U 3.2, 5.4.3].
- Without the battery the system clock is wrong after power-up [U 2]. The
  8056 timestamps (ms since 1970) are only as good as that clock; 3.19.2 can
  follow an NTP server (`set_ntpServer`) [S 2.2.8.4].
- The core board runs Linux with an sFTP server: JBI programs live in
  /rbctrl, Lua scripts in /rbctrl/luadir (root/elite2014, port 22) [U 6.7.1,
  9] [J preface].

### 4.2 Software inside

- The motion controller: planning and interpolation of every motion type
  (§5.7), kinematics (forward, inverse with a reference, all inverse
  solutions), a dynamics model identified per robot and per payload (used for
  gravity compensation, hand guiding, collision detection, force estimates)
  [U 6.11, 12.3] [S 2.2.4]. The process seems to be called "mcserver" (alarm
  "can't connect to mcserver") [Sv 4.1] [I].
- Safety logic and configuration (§5.8); kinematic parameters: the minimum
  and maximum joint speeds, linear speeds and rotation speeds that bound every
  motion, and the "absolute" speeds AV and AVR [U 12.3.1] [J 2.4.7].
- Programs: JBI, the only language with motion instructions; up to eight Lua
  scripts in the background; a soft PLC (ladder, 200 rungs) that maps inputs
  to "safety relays" such as external start, servo on, clear alarm, encoder
  calibration, reduced mode [U 6.7, 9, 10] [J].
- Variables kept through power-down: B, I, D (numbers), P (joint positions),
  V (poses), 256 of each [U 6.8.3.3] [J 2.1].
- Fieldbus slaves: Modbus RTU/TCP (port 502), Profinet, EtherNet/IP, CC-Link
  (optional) [D] [C].
- Logs: the pendant's information log, which is not written to disk in real
  time ("if the robot is immediately powered off after the alarm occurs, the
  latest alarm message may be lost") [U 6.5.4]; served on 8058 and 8059 [S 4,
  5].

### 4.3 I/O and the virtual bits

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| Safety inputs | X0/X1 emergency stop, X2/X3 safeguard stop: dual channel, low active [U 7.4, 14.1] |
| Box | DI X4–X19, DO Y0–Y19 (NPN), AI 1–2 and AO 1–4 (±10 V, 12 bit), RS-485 (up to 2 Mbit/s), Ethernet [U 7] |
| Tool flange (12 pins) | DI X48/X49, DO Y48/Y49 (sinking, 1 A), AI 3 and AO 5 (0–10 V), RS-485 (2–4 ms latency each way), 24 V; two buttons (X50 blue, X51 green: drag, record or disabled) [U 7.7, 6.11.1] |
| Virtual bits M0–M1535 | the controller's flags and the PLC's relays; M0–M527 are the system's; M528–M799 free for users [U 8, 14.1] [J 2.1] |

System flags worth reading (`getVirtualOutput`, `getRegisters`) [U 14.1]
[S 2.2.5]: M416/M417/M418 TEACH/PLAY/REMOTE; M400 servo enabled; M424 stop,

collision alarm; M472 precise; M473 initialised; M474 backdrive; M475
collision detection on; M476 ready to switch the servo on; M49 safeguard
stop; M526/M527 reduced mode.

## 5. Behaviour

### 5.1 Modes

The key switch on the pendant sets the mode [U 3.3.1, 6.2] [S 2.2.2.2]:

| Mode | Who moves the arm | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TEACH (0) | the pendant: jog keys with the enabling switch held in its middle position, hand guiding with the flange button | settings, programs and variables can only be changed here; moving from PLAY to TEACH ends the running program |
| PLAY (1) | programs started from the pendant (servo button, start, pause) | |
| REMOTE (2) | "an external method that does not use the teach pendant", e.g. the SDK over Ethernet | every SDK call that moves or changes something requires it (§6.4) |

The driver can read the mode, not set it: turning the key is the
operator's.

### 5.2 Power, enable and readiness

| Step | Read | Make it so (SDK, REMOTE) | On the pendant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arm powered | `get_robot_power_status` (V3.12.2+: 0 off, 1 powering on, 2 on) | `set_robot_power_status 1` (V3.12.2+) [S 2.2.2.87-88] | power switch, then automatic |
| Initialised, precise | `get_servo_precise_position_status`; M472, M473 | `calibrate_encoder_zero_position` (servo on) [S 2.2.3.21] | enabling switch + "Imprecise", or move each axis |
| No alarm | `getRobotState` ≠ 4, `get_servo_alarm_state`, `getAlarmNum` | `clearAlarm`; `reset_robot_status` (not after an emergency stop) [S 2.2.1.4, 2.2.11.5] | Reset |
| Synchronised | `getMotorStatus`; 8056 `can_motor_run` | `syncMotorStatus` | reset and synchronise |
| Servo on (brakes released) | `getServoStatus`; 8056 `servoReady`; M400 | `set_servo_status 1` (`0` engages the brakes) | servo button (PLAY), enabling switch (TEACH) |
| Ready to move | all of the above, in REMOTE, state stop | | |

A joint alarm right after power-up is normal: servo alarm 201, "joint is not
ready, initialization is not complete ... a normal alarm; click reset"
[Sv 4.2].

### 5.3 States

`getRobotState` and 8056 report [S 2.2.2.1, 3.1]: 0 stop, 1 pause, 2
emergency stop, 3 running, 4 alarm, 5 collision (8056 reports collision in
its own byte). The emergency-stop state "will only exist for a short time and
will be covered by the alarm soon": use `get_estop_status` or 8056's
`emergencyStopState` to see it [S 2.2.2.1, 2.2.2.61]. A motion has ended when
the state is back to stop; the manual's `wait_stop` polls `getRobotState`
every 10 ms until 0 or 4 [S 2.2.7.6, 2.2.9.10].

### 5.4 Stops

| Stop | Triggered by | What happens | Back to work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency stop | pendant button, external E_STOP inputs, PLC S0 | stop category 1: a controlled stop, then the motors' power is cut; the program stops; the arm may sag a little as the brakes engage [U 1.7, 7.4, Table 7-2] [Sv 2.1.2] | remove the cause, release the button, reset the alarm, synchronise; program call stacks are lost [U 6.8.2] [Sv servo alarm 40] |
| Safeguard stop | X2/X3 (e.g. a door) | category 2: a controlled stop, power stays on; the program pauses [U 7.4.3, Table 7-2] | the green start button, or automatically if configured (PLC S19, M48/M525) |
| Pause | pendant pause, `pause` [S 2.2.3.11], PAUSE in JBI | the program pauses at its line; the arm slows down and stops [U 6.8.2] | `run` / start |
| Stop | `stop` [S 2.2.3.9], double click on pause | the motion or program ends | |
| Alarm | any fault during motion | "the program execution will end, and the manipulator will stop immediately" [U 6.8.2] | clear the alarm |
| Collision | collision detection | state 5 | `resetCollisionState`, then clear [S 2.2.2.35] |
| Family stops | `stop_trajectory`, `tt_clear_servo_joint_buf`, `stopj`, `stopl`; jog stops 1 s after the last `jog` | the family's motion ends [S 2.2.3.8, 2.2.3.17-19, 2.2.7.5, 2.2.9.8] | |

Measured stopping on an emergency stop, arm stretched, 6 kg, at 100 %
speed: about 0.18–0.22 rad and 0.145 s for J1–J3 (at 10 %: 0.002 rad, 0.018
s) [U Tables 7-4, 7-5]. An "emergency stop deceleration stop time", "how long
the system takes to respond to an emergency stop", is a parameter of 1–200
ms [U 12.3.2].

### 5.5 Speed

- Three speed settings: the manual (TEACH) speed, and one shared by PLAY and
  REMOTE [U 6.5.12]. In REMOTE it is `setSpeed`/`getSpeed`, 0.05–100 %
  [S 2.2.3.15, 2.2.2.34]; the pendant shows 0.05–30 % green, 31–70 % grey,
  71–100 % red.
- "When the speed type is VJ, V, VR ... the robot runs at speed = (speed
  parameter) × (overall speed % set on the teach pendant). When the speed type
  is AV, AVR, the robot runs at the set value" [J 2.4]. So `moveByJoint`'s
  speed (a percentage) and the V and VR types of `moveByLine`/`Arc`/`Rotate`
  (mm/s, deg/s) are multiplied by the slider; AV and AVR are not [I for the
  SDK calls, which mirror the JBI instructions].
- Speeds outside the kinematic parameters are clamped to them, not refused
  [J 2.4.7 note].
- Whether the slider also scales `start_trajectory` and transparent
  transmission is unknown (§8).

### 5.6 Frames, tools, payload

- Poses are x, y, z [mm] and rx, ry, rz [rad, or degrees with `unit_type`
  0], composed R = Rz(rz)·Ry(ry)·Rx(rx), and `poseMul(p1, p2)` = T1·T2 [M,
  with tools/rotation_convention.py, 2026-10-11]. The JBI manual calls rx, ry, rz "the rotation vector" [J 2.1]:
  our measurement fits the fixed-axis angles to 5·10⁻¹¹ and rules that out.
- Eight tools (0–7): the TCP offset from the flange (x, y, z within ±500 mm,
  rx, ry, rz) and the payload's mass and centre of mass; a wrong payload
  spoils gravity compensation, hand guiding and collision detection [U 4.4,
  6.11.3-4] [S 2.2.2.19-20].
- Two current tool numbers: one for TEACH (`getToolNumber`) and one for PLAY
  and REMOTE (`getAutoRunToolNumber`) [S 2.2.2.10, 2.2.2.36].
- Eight user frames (0–7), taught by three points or set numerically
  [U 6.11.5] [S 2.2.2.22-24]; 3.19.2 adds non-orthogonal frames 8–15.
- The 8056 frame carries the TCP pose in the base frame and in the current
  user frame, and the flange's [S 3.1].

### 5.7 Motion the controller offers

| Family | SDK calls | Who plans and times | What the PC does | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Point-to-point | `moveByJoint`, `moveByLine`, `moveByArc`, `moveByRotate`, `moveByLineCoord` [S 2.2.3.1-4, 20] | the controller: speed, acceleration and deceleration as % (default 80), optional stop condition (`cond_*`, the JBI UNTIL) | one call per move, the arm at rest first; then wait | state back to stop |
| Waypoint path | `clearPathPoint`, `addPathPoint` × n, `moveByPath`, `getPathPointIndex` [S 2.2.3.5-7] | the controller, blending corners by a radius (the last must be 0); I/O triggers along the way | upload, start, wait | state back to stop |
| Jog | `jog` [S 2.2.3.8] | the controller | repeat the call while jogging | 1 s after the last call, or `stop` |
| Speed streaming | `moveBySpeedj`, `moveBySpeedl`, `stopj`, `stopl` [S 2.2.3.16-19] | the controller ramps to each velocity and holds it for `t` | send the next velocity before `t` runs out: the newest wins | `t` runs out, or `stopj`/`stopl` |
| Servo streaming (transparent transmission) | `transparent_transmission_init`, `tt_put_servo_joint_to_buf`, `tt_clear_servo_joint_buf` [S 2.2.7] | the controller plays a queue of joint targets, one every `t` (2–100 ms) after `lookahead` (10–1000 ms), smoothed | feed one target per period | `tt_clear_servo_joint_buf`; an empty queue: unknown (§8) |
| Timestamped trajectory | `start_push_pos`, `push_pos` × n, `stop_push_pos`, `check_trajectory`, `start_trajectory`, `pause`/`resume`/`stop_trajectory`, `flush_trajectory` [S 2.2.9] | the PC gives positions with times; the controller interpolates and plays them, times scaled by `speed_percent` | upload, start, wait | the trajectory's end; it can run again until flushed or replaced |
| Programs | `checkJbiExist`, `runJbi`, `getJbiState`, `pause`, `run`, `stop` [S 2.2.3.10-14] | the controller, including MOVML (an offline point file at a fixed interval) and drag replay [J 2.4] | start, watch | the program's end |
| Hand guiding | `drag_teach_switch` [S 2.2.2.17] | the controller (gravity compensation) | switch on and off | off |
| Force control | `start_force_mode`, `fc_act`, ... [S 2.2.16] | the controller, with a force sensor or its estimate | configure, then move | `end_force_mode` |

Every move is REMOTE only, and "before executing the above commands, please
make sure that the robot is in a stopped state. If the robot is running, send
the stop command first and wait for the robot to stop" [S 2.2.3.4].

[I] The timestamp service and MOVML are probably one mechanism: the
controller's alarms speak of "the timestamp in the trajectory motion
instruction" and of JOB files, and MOVML replays files whose header can be
"trajfile" [Sv 4.1] [J 2.4.5].

### 5.8 Safety functions

All of them are the controller's, configured on the pendant (some through
`setSafetyParams`, with a password) [U 7.4, 12.2] [S 2.2.2.33]:

- emergency stop and safeguard stop, dual channel, PLd [U Table 7-2];
- collision detection, on or off, sensitivity 10–100 %; it needs a valid
  dynamics identification ("status green") and "is not a guarantee of
  safety" [U 1.7, 6.11.2.1, 12.2.4]; MCWAIT suspends it for a while
  [J 2.3.7];
- limits for power (80–1500 W), momentum (5–90 kg·m/s), tool force and elbow
  force (100–400 N) and speed (%), in a normal and a reduced mode, the
  reduced one entered through PLC signals S17/S18 [U 12.2.3] [S 2.2.2.33];
- soft joint limits, interference zones (16: boxes in the base frame or
  joint ranges, reported as M440–M455, signals only), joint speed limits
  (alarm "joint %s speed exceeds limit") [U 6.11.6, 12.3.3] [Sv 4.1].

### 5.9 Alarms and logs

- Alarm numbers look like 0-1030-ca: a level (0, 1 or 2: error, warning,
  information [I], matching the log's three types [S 4]), a group, an index
  [Sv 4]. Program alarms: 1000 system, 1030–1033 motion and robot state,
  1800 servo, 2001–2002 JBI, 4000 calibration, 6000 Lua, 7000 trajectory
  planning, 9000 palletising. Servo drive alarms carry causes and remedies
  [Sv 4.2].
- `getAlarmNum` returns the numbers of the last five alarms [S 2.2.2.56];
  8058 serves the decoded log (send `Type=Info|Warning|Error`, then `all`, a
  line count, or `exit`), 8059 the raw log [S 4, 5]. Both exist on 3.11.2
  [S-zh 1].
- In an alarm the controller slows its communication: the Modbus cycle goes
  from 10 ms to 1010 ms [C 2.4.1]. Whether 8055 and 8056 slow down too is
  unknown [I].

### 5.10 Starting and ending a session

**On the pendant** [U 4.2-4.3, 4.7, 6.1]: key into the power switch, turn it
on; initialise and calibrate (precise); reset alarms; mount and tool set;
turn the key to the mode; servo on. To end: turn the key from PLAY or REMOTE
to TEACH (this ends the running program), shut down from the pendant, and
switch the power off once the robot has stopped.

**Over the SDK, in REMOTE.** Starting is the vendor's examples [S 2.2.9.10,
2.2.7.6, 2.3.1]: `getRobotState`, and `clearAlarm` if 4 (then 0.5 s);
`getMotorStatus`, and `syncMotorStatus` if not synchronised (then 0.5 s);
0.5 s; `getServoStatus`, and `set_servo_status 1` if off (then about 1 s).
Precise is the pendant's start-up step; over the SDK it can be read and
calibrated (§5.2). The manuals give no sequence for ending a session; the
pieces are: end the motion with its family's stop, wait for state stop,
`set_servo_status 0` (the brakes engage), and on V3.12.2 or later
`set_robot_power_status 0` (the arm's power off) [S 2.2.2.87]. The key
switch and the box's power stay with the operator.

**The servo between motions.** Leave it on. The controller holds the arm
still under servo control as long as needed; every servo-on releases and
checks the brakes, a second or so with a small motion [I, from §3.3]; the
brakes are holding brakes, for power-off and emergencies. Switch the servo
off at the end of a session, or before leaving the arm unattended. Other
cobots work the same way: on a UR, brakes and arm power are a separate step
from programs, and ur_rtde does not power the arm down when a program ends
[I].

## 6. The SDK as an interface

### 6.1 Ports

| Port | What | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| 8055 | control: every call | JSON-RPC 2.0, one JSON object per line, both ways [S 2] |
| 8056 | monitor: state frames | 1024-byte frames, big-endian, about every 8 ms [S 3] |
| 8058 | log, decoded | text commands and lines [S 4] |
| 8059 | log, raw (2.14.0+) | text [S 5] |
| 502 | Modbus TCP slave | M bits and read-only robot registers [C 2] |
| 6680 | web pendant | HTTP [U 11.6] |
| 22 | sFTP | files in /rbctrl [U 6.7.1] |

### 6.2 The command channel (8055)

- Requests carry an id that the reply echoes. The result is a JSON value
  wrapped in a string (`"result":"[2.08,...]"`). Errors: −32601 method not
  found; −32693 for "parameters not found, out of range, execution conditions
  not met" [S 2].
- The vendor's examples send one request and wait for its reply before the
  next [S 2.1]. Two of them do not wait (`response_enable 0` for streaming,
  `ret_flag 0` for an upload) [S 2.2.7.7, 2.2.9.11].
- [M] Some replies take well over 50 ms (`set_servo_status`, 2026-10-14).
  Unconfirmed `push_pos`, 20 every 8 ms, backed up on the EA66; with the
  clean-up and the disconnect that followed, 8055 and 8056 refused
  connections until the controller's power was cut, and the pendant asked to
  contact support (2026-10-14). Never send unconfirmed bursts.
- Calls that read a serial port wait up to their timeout (default 100 ms)
  [S 2.2.14.3]; with one call in flight, they hold the channel that long [I].
- Whether a second client may connect to 8055 or 8056 at the same time is
  unknown (§8).

### 6.3 The monitor stream (8056)

- A long-lived connection: "if the access time exceeds 8 ms, the data will
  pile up in the buffer" [S 3], so a reader must keep reading.
- Each frame [S 3.1]: size, timestamp (ms since 1970, the controller's
  clock), cycle mode, joint angles [deg], TCP pose in the base and in the
  user frame, motor torques (per mille), robot state (0–4), servo ready,
  synchronised (`can_motor_run`), motor speeds [rpm], mode, analog and
  digital I/O, collision, flange poses, emergency stop, TCP speed and
  acceleration, joint speeds and accelerations, joint temperatures, joint
  torques [Nm], estimated external joint torques and TCP force, drag state,
  force sensor connected; 219 reserved bytes; a check word 0xec8056ec.

### 6.4 What needs REMOTE

Queries and computations work in any mode. "This command is only supported
in remote mode" is noted on every call that moves the arm or changes the
controller: servo, synchronisation, clearing alarms, tool and user numbers
and data, payload, cycle mode, collision reset, every motion family, `stop`,
`run`, `pause`, `runJbi`, `setSpeed`, outputs, variables, transparent
transmission, the timestamp service, backdrive, force control [S 2.2].
`setSafetyParams` works in REMOTE and PLAY [S 2.2.2.33].

### 6.5 Versions

The driver types the 3.11.2 set, our firmware's generation. 3.19.2 adds,
among others: the arm's power (`set_robot_power_status`, 3.12.2), welding
(3.12.2), the Mini's brake voltage (3.14.2), force-controlled dragging
(3.14.2), hand guiding in a pause (3.16.2), NTP and time zone, pose targets
for `moveByJoint` and `moveByLine`, non-orthogonal user frames [S]. Calls
from 3.12.2 to 3.15.x should work on V3.15.3 [I].
